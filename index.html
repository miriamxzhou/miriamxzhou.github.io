Research

Selected projects from my undergraduate work at Pomona College and the Claremont Colleges, grouped by theme.

Supply chains & networks

Jun–Aug 2025

Pandemic-induced supply chain network optimization

Kenneth Cooke Summer Research Fellowship · Advisor: Adolfo J. Rumbos

A multi-tier equilibrium model of global supply chains built on variational inequality theory, extended with stochastic demand and costs. It shows how cost asymmetries and uncertainty amplify inefficiency when decisions are decentralized.

May–Jul 2024

Graph Laplacians and network connectivity

Pomona College · Advisor: Adolfo J. Rumbos

How Laplacian eigenvalues and algebraic connectivity relate to the mixing time of random walks, studied through spectral decomposition and simulation in Python.

Healthcare & public policy

2025–2026

Modeling measles dynamics and vaccination policy impacts

MuddOR Lab, Harvey Mudd College · Advisors: Susan Martonosi, Banafsheh Behzad, Ruben A. Proano

How socioeconomic disparities and vaccine hesitancy shape outbreak intensity across North America. Uninsured rates strongly predict outbreak intensity in the U.S., while behavioral and access factors dominate in Canada.

May–Jun 2025

Vote-by-mail reversal and partisan and minority participation

Claremont McKenna College Policy Lab · Advisor: Zachary Courser

Processed 3 TB of voter registration records on a high-performance computing cluster to find inconsistencies and demographic anomalies that bias election policy analysis.

Applied econometrics

Jan–May 2026

Spatial inequality and neighborhood wage disparities in Los Angeles

Senior thesis, Pomona College · Advisor: Fernando Lozano

A tract-level study of LA County combining ACS, county income and GIS data with geographic fixed effects. Education, poverty and labor-market conditions explain much of the variation, but localized disparities persist.

May–Jun 2025

The Big Mac Index as a proxy for food-sector inflation

Pomona College · Advisor: Kyle Wilson

A 2012–2025 panel for the U.S., China and Malaysia, estimated with OLS and Newey–West errors. Big Mac prices track fast-food inflation in the U.S. but diverge elsewhere because of structural pricing differences.
